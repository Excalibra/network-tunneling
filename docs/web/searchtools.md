## Ask

> The distinctive feature of its search engine is comparatively strong support for semantic search, while also incorporating traditional keyword search; in technical implementation it lies between Google and [W|A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Alpha).
>
> After the merger of Yahoo’s and Microsoft’s search businesses, Ask.com ranked as the third-largest public search engine in the United States and the sixth-largest in the world, behind only Google, Bing, Baidu, [NAVER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naver) and [Yandex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yandex). According to one survey report, Ask.com is the preferred alternative search engine among Google users.
>
> —— Excerpt from Wikipedia

## DuckDuckGo

> DuckDuckGo is an internet search engine headquartered in Paoli, Pennsylvania, United States. DuckDuckGo emphasises the incorporation of content from major Web 2.0 sites on the foundation of traditional search engines, advocates the protection of users’ privacy rights, and undertakes not to monitor or record users’ search content.

## Ecosia

> Ecosia is a web search engine that donates at least 80 % of its remaining revenue to public-interest causes and is currently focused on tree planting. Ecosia supports full financial transparency. Ecosia has been certified as a public-benefit corporation by B-labs, which states that “since its establishment in December 2009 the search engine has donated 80 % of its advertising revenue, raising more than 1.5 million dollars for the protection of rainforests”. It has released a free web browser called EcoBrowser (a modified version of Chromium).
>
>—— Excerpt from Wikipedia

The principal search source is Bing; local or restricted versions of Bing often return comparatively heavily filtered data

## Qwant

> **Qwant** is a French company that launched its eponymous web search engine in July 2013. It claims not to employ [user tracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Website_visitor_tracking) and not to personalise search results, thereby avoiding the trapping of users inside a [filter bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_bubble).
>
> The site processes more than 10 million search requests each day and serves more than 50 million individual users worldwide each month, distributed across three principal entry points: the ordinary home page, a simplified version, and the Qwant Junior portal that filters results for children.
>
> —— Excerpt from Wikipedia
